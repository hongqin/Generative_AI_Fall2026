# Final Project Report

*Graded discussion, 100 points.*

*Submission format in Canvas: reply to the discussion post. Your reply is the submission.*

## Purpose

This is the **Final Project Report**, the largest formal project milestone (100 points) and the culmination of the work that started with your Proposal in Module 2, continued through the Mid-Project Report in Module 5 and the Draft Report in Module 7. This report should be readable as a standalone account of your project: a reader who has not seen the prior reports should be able to follow what you did, what you found, and what is left to do.

### Submit by replying to this discussion

**Reply to this post to submit your work.** Canvas records your reply as your submission for grading. Attach your 1500-3000 word PDF to the reply.

### Required structure

Write a **1500 to 3000 word PDF** (roughly 5 to 10 pages with figures and references) organized into the following eight sections in this order:

1.  **Title and team.** Final project title; team members; the model family or families used.
2.  **Abstract (~150 words)**: a self-contained one-paragraph summary of the problem, approach, and main results.
3.  **Introduction**: the problem you addressed, why it matters, what your approach was, what you found at a high level. ~300-400 words.
4.  **Related work**: 5 to 10 primary citations situating your work. Brief; this is not a review article.
5.  **Methods**: data, model architecture, training procedure, evaluation procedure. Be specific enough that a classmate could reproduce your project from this section.
6.  **Results**: quantitative and qualitative findings. Include 4 to 6 figures or tables. Be specific about the evaluation set, sampling procedure, and metric implementation.
7.  **Discussion**: what worked, what did not, what surprised you, how the results compare to prior work. The most distinctive section; this is where your judgment shows.
8.  **Limitations and future work**: honest accounting of what your project does not do. What would you tackle with another month? Another year?
9.  **References**: a full reference list (any standard format).

### Code and reproducibility

Attach one of the following to your discussion reply, in addition to the PDF:

- A link to a public GitHub (or similar) repository containing your code, with a README that explains how to reproduce the main results.
- OR a zip file of your notebooks and supporting code with a README.

Reproducibility-grade code is part of the rubric.

### Peer engagement

Reply to **at least one classmate’s Final Project Report** with a substantive comment: a question about their methods, a suggestion for future work, a related paper or dataset, or a comparison to your own project.

### Evaluation Criteria (100 points)

- **20 pts:** Methods section: data, model, training, evaluation; specific and reproducibility-grade.
- **25 pts:** Results section: quantitative and qualitative; 4 to 6 figures or tables; specific evaluation methodology.
- **20 pts:** Discussion: judgment, surprise, comparison to prior work; the section that distinguishes a strong report from a mediocre one.
- **10 pts:** Related work: appropriate citations; situates the project correctly.
- **10 pts:** Limitations and future work: honest, specific accounting.
- **10 pts:** Code and reproducibility: README explains how to reproduce; code runs end-to-end.
- **5 pts:** Peer reply (substantive).
