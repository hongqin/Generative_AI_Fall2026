# Recorded Research Paper Presentation

*Assignment, 100 points.*

## Purpose

This is the Recorded Research Paper Presentation, the formal application of the Module 1 Setup Discussion (where you proposed three primary-research-paper candidates). You will record a 12- to 15-minute video presentation of the primary research paper you chose, applying the structure and slide-design principles from Topic 4. The presentation is worth 100 points (10% of the course grade) and is graded against the rubric below.

### Task

Pick one primary research paper related to generative AI (this should be one of the candidates you proposed in the Module 1 Setup Discussion; if your project has shifted and you want to switch, send the instructor a Canvas Inbox message first). Prepare a slide deck. Record a 12- to 15-minute video presentation following the six-section structure from Topic 4: title and opener, problem and prior work, method, results, limitations and open questions, relevance to the course or your project.

#### Format requirements

- **Slide-based:** 10 to 20 slides (Google Slides, PowerPoint, Keynote, Canva, or similar). Record yourself walking through the slides; do not just talk to camera.
- **Length:** 12 to 15 minutes target; 16 minutes is the hard ceiling.
- **Recording tools:** ODU Panopto (recommended), Zoom local, Microsoft Teams, Loom, OBS, QuickTime, or your phone’s screen recorder are all acceptable.
- **Where to put the video:** upload directly to the Canvas assignment, OR attach a link to a hosted recording (Panopto, OneDrive, YouTube unlisted, Loom, etc.) with view permissions for your instructor and classmates.
- **Attach your slide deck** as a separate PDF.
- **Captions or transcript required.** Use the recording tool’s auto-captions (review for accuracy) or attach a script as a separate file.

#### Required content beats (the six-section structure from Topic 4)

1.  **Title and “why care” opener** (~1 min)
2.  **Problem and prior work** (~2-3 min)
3.  **The method** (~4-5 min): the longest section, with diagrams and equations
4.  **Results** (~2-3 min): the key empirical or theoretical findings
5.  **Limitations and open questions** (~1-2 min)
6.  **Relevance to the course or your project** (~1 min)

### Evaluation Criteria (100 points)

- **25 pts:** Problem framing and motivation: clearly explains what the paper is solving and why it matters, in language a classmate from outside the sub-area can follow.
- **25 pts:** Method explanation: walks through the technical contribution with diagrams and equations; equations are explained, not just shown.
- **20 pts:** Results and limitations: presents the main results clearly AND covers what the paper does not do (limitations section is required, not optional).
- **15 pts:** Slide quality: readable fonts, one-idea-per-slide structure, citations on borrowed figures.
- **10 pts:** Timing, pace, and audio quality: between 12 and 15 minutes; pauses give the audience time to read; audio is clear.
- **5 pts:** Captions or transcript present.
