# Week 1, Topic 3: Course Workflow, Submissions, Communication, and Project Expectations

## Overview

This topic is the “how the course actually runs” reference. It pulls together four operational areas so you have one place to look up workflow questions during the term: **Course Workflow**, **Submissions**, **Communication**, and **Project Expectations**. Bookmark this page.

### 1. Course Workflow

This course is eight weeks long, fully asynchronous, and delivered through Canvas. **All modules are open from the start of the term and stay open through the end.** Due dates are the recommended weekly cadence, not hard locks; if you fall behind one week you can still access subsequent weeks. Late submissions still receive a **5 percent deduction per day** per the syllabus, so on-time work matters.

Recommended cadence for each weekly module:

1.  Read the module Overview and the four Topic pages.
2.  Do the hands-on or coding portion of the weekly technical assignment.
3.  Post your weekly technical assignment reply.
4.  Post your project-track update for the week.
5.  Reply to at least one classmate on either discussion.

Plan for roughly **12 to 15 hours per week**, including lectures, readings, the weekly assignment, the project update, and steady progress on your course project. In an eight-week course, missing one week is roughly equivalent to missing two weeks in a regular sixteen-week semester.

### 2. Submissions

Every graded item in this course is submitted through Canvas. The exact submission mechanism depends on the item type:

#### Weekly Technical Assignments (40% of course grade)

Submitted via the Canvas assignment in each module. The accepted format is specified inside each assignment, and may include: a short PDF write-up, a Jupyter notebook (.ipynb), a zipped code folder, a link to a public repository with clear run instructions, or screenshots and figures embedded in a text submission. Expect to interpret your results, not just paste raw output.

#### Course Project Track (40% of course grade)

**Every project-track deliverable in Modules 1 through 8 is a graded discussion.** To submit, you reply to the discussion post in the module; Canvas records your reply as the submission. Attach PDFs (proposal, mid-project report, draft, final report), notebooks, or repository links to the reply. The same rubric language applies whether your submission is short (informal check-ins in Modules 1, 3, 4, 6) or substantial (formal milestones in Modules 2, 5, 7, 8).

#### Research Paper Presentation (10% of course grade)

Submitted in Module 7 as a recorded video. Upload the video file directly, attach a link to a hosted recording (Panopto, YouTube, OneDrive video, etc.), and attach your slide deck as a separate file. Plan for 12 to 15 minutes of presentation time per the syllabus.

#### Course Project Presentation (10% of course grade)

Submitted in Module 8 as the *Recorded Final Project Presentation* assignment, separate from the Final Project Report. Same upload conventions as the paper presentation.

#### File format conventions

- Written work: PDF preferred; Word is accepted.
- Code: a notebook (.ipynb) or a zipped folder; a link to a public GitHub repository is also fine if the README explains how to run it.
- Video: upload directly to Canvas, or attach a link to Panopto / YouTube / a similar hosted location. Provide captions or a transcript for accessibility.

#### Late policy

Per the syllabus, late work receives a **5 percent deduction per day**. Work submitted more than one week late may receive reduced or no credit unless we have made prior arrangements. If illness, work travel, family responsibilities, or another serious circumstance is going to affect a deadline, message the instructor through Canvas Inbox *before* the due date if at all possible.

#### Academic integrity and use of AI tools

The full AI-use expectation is on the Meet Your Instructor page. In short: AI tools are encouraged for learning, debugging, and drafting, but you are responsible for the correctness, the originality, and the integrity of everything you submit, and for being able to explain your work in your own words. ODU’s academic-integrity policies and honor pledge apply in full.

### 3. Communication

#### Reaching the instructor

- **Canvas Inbox** is the preferred channel for any course-related question. Response target: one business day on weekdays.
- **ODU email** (`hqin@odu.edu`) is the right channel for personal or sensitive matters (illness, accommodations, conflicts) if you prefer email over Canvas Inbox.
- **Student Hours** are held weekly by Zoom; the standing time is posted in Canvas Announcements at the start of the term. Ad hoc meetings by Zoom or Microsoft Teams can be scheduled through Canvas Inbox.

#### Class-wide questions

Post general questions whose answers might help your classmates (clarifications about an assignment, debugging help on a common tool, conceptual questions about a topic) to the **Q&A Café Discussion Forum** in the Start Here module rather than messaging the instructor privately. Your peers will often answer faster, and the answer becomes a resource everyone benefits from.

#### Project discussions

Your project-track discussion threads in Modules 1 through 8 are also a communication channel. Replying to your own posts to document progress, and replying briefly to peers, both count toward weekly engagement.

#### Announcements

The instructor will post Announcements in Canvas at least weekly. You are expected to check Canvas at least twice a week. ODU email forwarding for Canvas notifications is recommended.

### 4. Project Expectations

#### What the project is

You will design, develop, document, and present a generative-AI project applied to a real problem, dataset, or research question (CLO3). The project topic must be related to **Generative AI and/or Agentic AI**. You may work individually or in **instructor-approved small teams**; team approval happens before the project proposal is finalized.

#### Project cadence

The project runs the full term. You are expected to build it incrementally; do not back-load the work into the final two weeks. There are **four formal graded milestones** and **four informal weekly check-ins**, all delivered as graded discussions:

- **Module 1 (informal):** Setup Confirmation and Project-Interest Note (your initial 2 to 3 project candidates).
- **Module 2 (formal milestone):** Course Project Proposal.
- **Module 3 (informal):** Implementation Check-in.
- **Module 4 (informal):** Progress Update with Initial Implementation Notes.
- **Module 5 (formal milestone):** Mid-Project Report.
- **Module 6 (informal):** Methods Refinement Note.
- **Module 7 (formal milestone):** Draft Report or Progress Report.
- **Module 8 (formal milestone):** Final Project Report and Recorded Final Project Presentation.

#### Encouraged alignment between paper and project

The Module 1 setup discussion asks you to propose three primary-research-paper candidates for your Module 7 recorded presentation. **Ideally at least one of your three paper candidates should be related to one of your project topics.** The deeper you read for the paper presentation, the better positioned you will be to implement, extend, evaluate, or critique that line of work in your course project.

#### Deliverables across the term

- A clearly written project proposal with problem statement, methods, data, and references.
- A mid-project report documenting what you have built, what you have tested, feedback received, and revised next steps.
- A draft or progress report in Module 7 showing implementation, results, and remaining work.
- A final written report and supporting code (or a reproducibility-grade repository).
- A recorded final project presentation (12 to 15 minutes).

#### Evaluation criteria

The syllabus rubric weights project scope, technical soundness, evidence of progress, use of appropriate methods, reproducibility, interpretation of results, and quality of written and spoken communication. Detailed rubrics live with each formal milestone discussion.
